# Governance

Kalix is an open-source project led by one person, with a small core team and
an open contribution process. This document says who decides what, and what
happens if the lead cannot continue.

## Roles

**Project lead.** Chas Egan (@chasegan). The lead sets the project's
direction and scope, decides what is merged, and is the final word on design
questions. The lead holds the Kalix trade mark, the kalix.org domain, and
administers the repository, the kalix-org organisation, and the project's
hosted services.

**Core team.** Chas Egan and Ciaran Komarakul-Greene. Core team members
review and merge contributions, triage issues, and answer questions in
Discussions. They act within the direction the lead has set; anything that
would change that direction goes to the lead.

**Contributors.** Anyone who opens an issue, answers a question, improves the
documentation, or submits a pull request. Contributors do not need permission
to start; they need to read [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) first.

**Advisory group.** Craig Johansen, Owen Droop, Sanne Voogt, Joel Rutten, and
Mackenzie Edwards. The advisory group has no operational role day to day. Its
purpose is described under Succession.

## How decisions are made

Most decisions are small and are made in the pull request that proposes them.
Larger ones, in roughly ascending order of ceremony:

- **Bug fixes and small improvements** are reviewed and merged by a core team
  member.
- **New features and behavioural changes** begin as an issue describing the
  problem and the proposed approach. A core team member agrees the approach
  before code is written.
- **Design decisions** that shape the architecture are recorded as
  [Architecture Decision Records](https://kalix.org/code/design/adrs/). An ADR
  is accepted by the lead.
- **Scope**, meaning what Kalix is and is not, is set by the
  [manifesto](https://kalix.org/code/design/manifesto/). Changes to the
  manifesto are made by the lead and are rare.

There is no vote. The lead listens, decides, and explains. Disagreement is
welcome in the issue or ADR; once a decision is made, the discussion moves on.

## Funding and priority

Organisations that fund development buy the lead's time to work on things
within the roadmap and the manifesto. Funding sets priority among acceptable
work; it does not extend what is acceptable, and it does not confer a veto.
Funders are told this before money changes hands, so that no one is
surprised later.

## Succession

If Chas Egan is unable to continue as lead, for any reason and for an
extended period, Ciaran Komarakul-Greene becomes project lead. The advisory
group exists for this moment: its members are people who know the project,
its users, and its founder's intentions, and the new lead is expected to
consult them during the transition and for as long afterwards as is useful.

Administrative access to the repository, the kalix-org organisation, the
domain, and hosted services is held by at least two people at all times so
that the transfer is a matter of hours, not a legal process.

The trade mark and domain are personal property of the lead and pass under
the lead's will or other legal arrangements. The lead's recorded intention is
that they follow the project, and the lead has made arrangements to that
effect.

## Changing this document

The lead may change this document at any time. Changes are committed to the
repository with an explanatory message so that the history is visible.
